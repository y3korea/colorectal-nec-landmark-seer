## Main figures

Conventions for all figures. The conditional 12-month probability of cancer death is the Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence of death from the index cancer (SEER cause-specific death classification; other-cause death as competing event; unknown cause coded as other cause) within 12 months after each landmark, among patients alive and under follow-up at that landmark. Confidence intervals (CIs) are 95% patient-level percentile bootstrap intervals with the same resample at every landmark (B = 2,000 for NEC cohorts; B = 500 for the large comparator cohorts where stated); between-cohort contrasts use independent bootstraps paired by resample index. pp = percentage points. Filled markers denote 100 or more patients at risk at the landmark and open markers fewer than 100; numbers at risk are patients alive and under follow-up at the landmark. Figures are 180 mm wide, Arial, vector PDF plus 300-dpi PNG, in the Okabe–Ito color-blind-safe palette with series additionally distinguished by marker shape and line style so that they read in grayscale print. NEC = neuroendocrine carcinoma; SCLC = small-cell lung cancer; LCNEC = large-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma; RR = rate ratio; TOST = two one-sided tests.

**Figure 1. Conditional 12-month probability of cancer death in colorectal NEC: landmark curves, plateau description, dependence on the window width and equivalence framing.**
(A) Monthly landmark curves (landmarks 0–24 months; 12-month window) for distant-stage (n = 908), regional (n = 346) and localized (n = 116) colorectal NEC, with 95% bootstrap bands (B = 2,000) and numbers at risk. The regional and localized curves are imprecise and their early changes do not establish a plateau (plateau-end intervals reach 0). Monthly landmarks are exploratory (added after the pre-specified landmarks in Table 2 had been seen).
(B) Distant-stage NEC only. Blue: unadjusted Aalen–Johansen curve with 95% band as in (A). Black dashed with crosses: directly standardized curve from per-landmark multi-state Cox models (cause-specific hazards for cancer and other death; covariates age, age squared, sex, calendar year, large-cell morphology and rectal site), averaging the predicted conditional probability over the cohort's own covariate distribution; point estimates only. Its 95% patient-level bootstrap intervals (B = 500; added at revision) are given in Supplementary Table S40; in 206 of 500 replicates a late-landmark coefficient diverged, so those intervals are approximate. Orange: broken-stick fit to the unadjusted landmarks 0–18 (change point c searched on a 0.25-month grid from 0 to 15 months); the dotted line marks c = 6.75 months and the shaded band its 95% bootstrap interval (4.00–9.75 months; c = 0 in 0.15% of resamples); the slope after c is −2.46 pp per month (−3.90 to −1.37). Landmarks beyond 15 months rest on fewer than 100 patients; the 19 estimates share patients across overlapping windows and are autocorrelated, so the hinge fit is descriptive.
(C) Change in the conditional probability of cancer death between landmark 6 and landmark 0 months (later minus earlier; negative = decline) as a function of the width of the risk window after each landmark (3, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 24 months) for distant-stage colorectal NEC (B = 2,000), SCLC (n = 77,707), colorectal adenocarcinoma (n = 147,014) and pancreatic adenocarcinoma (n = 75,634; comparators B = 500); added at revision. Error bars are 95% bootstrap intervals; points are offset horizontally; the shaded column marks the pre-specified 12-month window. Under a 3-month window the NEC curve falls from landmark 0 to 3 and rises from 3 to 6 (values in the text), so the flat 12-month curve is the running integral of a non-monotone short-term hazard, and the change point drifts from 11.75 to 5.75 months across windows (Supplementary Table S13). Both adenocarcinomas decline under every window.
(D) Equivalence (TOST) framing of the within-NEC landmark contrasts 6 − 0, 3 − 0, 6 − 3 and, for contrast, 12 − 0 months (distant-stage NEC; B = 2,000); added at revision. Thick segments are 90% bootstrap intervals (the TOST criterion at α = 0.05 is that the 90% interval lies inside the margin), thin segments 95% intervals, circles the point estimates. Vertical lines mark the post hoc margins of ±5 pp (solid, primary; shaded) and ±3 pp (dashed). TOST P values are bootstrap approximations (the larger of the two one-sided tail proportions; resolution 1/2,000, rounded half up). The 6 − 0, 3 − 0 and 6 − 3 contrasts are equivalent to no change within ±5 pp (P = 0.011, 0.003, 0.005) and ±4 pp (P = 0.033, 0.013, 0.015) but not within ±3 pp (P = 0.080, 0.060, 0.056); the 12 − 0 contrast (−12.3 pp) is non-equivalent at every margin. The intervals printed in the panel come from the equivalence bootstrap (seed 20261007), an independent draw from that of Table 2, and differ from the Table 2 bounds by at most 0.9 pp (Supplementary Methods S2). The margins were not part of the analysis protocol of September 28, 2026.

**Figure 2. Hazard of cancer death over time in distant-stage colorectal NEC and comparators.**
(A) Crude cancer-death rates per person-month in the intervals 0–3, 3–6, 6–9, 9–12, 12–15, 15–18 and 18–24 months after diagnosis in distant-stage colorectal NEC (n = 908; cancer deaths/person-months 304/2,118, 129/1,419, 128/952, 73/606, 51/414, 24/286 and 23/356); horizontal bars are the rates, shaded boxes exact Poisson 95% CIs (other-cause deaths censored at death). The annotation gives the adjusted RR for 6–9 versus 3–6 months from a piecewise-exponential Poisson model with person-month offset, cluster-robust standard errors by patient and covariates age/10, (age/10) squared, sex, calendar year/10, large-cell morphology and rectal site: RR 1.49 (95% CI 1.17–1.90), P = 0.0013; Holm-adjusted P = 0.0027 over the three post hoc contrasts and 0.025 over all 21 pairwise interval contrasts; a non-increasing rate after month 3 is rejected (parametric-bootstrap P = 0.006).
(B) Kernel-smoothed cause-specific hazard of cancer death (Epanechnikov kernel, boundary-corrected, R package muhaz) over 0–24 months in distant-stage colorectal NEC (n = 908), SCLC (n = 77,707), colorectal adenocarcinoma (n = 147,014) and pancreatic adenocarcinoma (n = 75,634), all at a common global bandwidth of 1.5 months (added at revision) to remove the bandwidth confound of automatic selection (automatic bandwidths 1.48, 0.34, 0.32 and 0.34 months). The blue band is the pointwise 95% percentile interval for NEC from 500 patient-level resamples. The boundary points at 0 and 24 months are not drawn. At this bandwidth NEC has a trough at month 4 (0.084 per person-month) and a post-trough maximum at 8.75 months (0.146; ratio of the hazards at these fixed months 1.74, 95% CI 1.32–2.35; ratio of the mean hazard over months 6–9 to that over months 3–6, trapezoid rule, 1.44, 1.18–1.75); SCLC shows the same trough–peak cycle (4.25 and 10.5 months; ratio 1.62); the two adenocarcinomas show no material post-trough rise (ratios 1.07 and 1.02). The automatic-bandwidth ratios in the text (1.75 and 1.45) are the primary estimates; the common-bandwidth values here are a comparator-matched sensitivity analysis. The timing of the trough and peak is imprecise (bootstrap intervals 3.5–11.5 and 6.75–24 months) and the hazard beyond month 16 rests on fewer than 100 NEC patients at risk.
(C) Chemotherapy-stratum RRs for 6–9, 9–15 and 15–24 months versus the reference interval 3–6 months among distant-stage NEC patients alive at 3 months (n = 553; chemotherapy recorded n = 465, no/unknown n = 88), from the same piecewise-exponential model with a chemotherapy × interval interaction; log scale, 95% Wald CIs with cluster-robust standard errors; global interaction test W = 7.57 on 3 df, P = 0.056. The SEER chemotherapy field is recorded whenever treatment is given and under-ascertains "no" (sensitivity about 68% against claims), so the stratum contrast is subject to treatment selection and residual immortal-time bias even after conditioning on 3-month survival; the baseline-classified decomposition of the plateau by chemotherapy (Supplementary Tables S22 and S30) is largely removed by landmark-specific exposure classification and is not interpreted as a treatment effect.

**Figure 3. Cross-tumor comparison of the conditional-risk trajectory in distant-stage disease.**
(A) Monthly landmark curves of the conditional 12-month probability of cancer death (landmarks 0–24) for distant-stage colorectal NEC (n = 908), SCLC (n = 77,707), colorectal adenocarcinoma (n = 147,014) and pancreatic adenocarcinoma (n = 75,634), with 95% bootstrap bands (B = 2,000 each; the comparator bands are narrower than the line width) and numbers at risk; markers every 3 months. Broken-stick plateau ends are 6.75, 8.50, 0 and 0 months, respectively. The SCLC and colorectal adenocarcinoma comparators were pre-specified on September 28, 2026 (pancreatic adenocarcinoma added sequentially after the first two comparators); lung LCNEC (panels B–D) was added at revision.
(B) Paired differences, NEC minus comparator, in the change of the conditional probability between landmarks (6 − 0, 12 − 6 and 12 − 0 months) for SCLC, lung LCNEC (n = 3,185), colorectal adenocarcinoma and pancreatic adenocarcinoma; 95% intervals from independent bootstraps paired by resample index (B = 2,000). Positive values mean that NEC declines less than the comparator. Over 0–6 months NEC differs from both adenocarcinomas and from lung LCNEC but not from SCLC (no difference detected; equivalent within ±5 pp, not within ±3 pp); over 6–12 months NEC falls at least as steeply as every comparator, so the distinctive feature is the shape of the first 6–9 months rather than a permanent difference in conditional risk.
(C) Two-by-two layout of the 0–6-month change by morphology (small-cell versus large-cell) and organ (colorectal versus lung): colorectal small-cell NEC (8041–8045, n = 599), colorectal large-cell NEC (8013, n = 309), SCLC (n = 77,707) and lung LCNEC (8013, n = 3,185), with 95% bootstrap CIs; added at revision. The pooled morphology effect (small-cell minus large-cell, averaged over organs) is +5.4 pp (95% CI 0.6 to 9.8), the organ effect +2.1 pp (−2.5 to 6.8) and the morphology × organ interaction +0.5 pp (−8.7 to 9.6). The colorectal large-cell cell is imprecise (n = 309); the evidence rests mainly on the contrast of lung small-cell versus large-cell (+5.1 pp, 2.8 to 7.5).
(D) Adjusted RR of cancer death in months 6–9 versus 3–6 by tumor (piecewise-exponential Poisson model, cluster-robust standard errors, log scale; covariates age/10, its square, sex and calendar year, plus rectal site for colorectal adenocarcinoma and morphology and rectal site for NEC): colorectal NEC (n = 908) 1.49 (1.17–1.90), SCLC (n = 77,707) 1.38 (1.34–1.42), lung LCNEC (n = 3,185; added at revision) 0.97 (0.84–1.11), colorectal adenocarcinoma (n = 147,014) 0.84 (0.82–0.86), pancreatic adenocarcinoma (n = 75,634) 0.87 (0.84–0.89). The panel footnote gives the 6-df Wald tests of the tumor × interval interaction (hazard-shape test) versus NEC in the pooled model over all years: SCLC P = 0.061; colorectal and pancreatic adenocarcinoma P < 0.001; lung LCNEC P = 0.018 (exploratory, not adjusted for multiplicity). The 2016–2023 tests are in Table 3; the morphology × organ layout of these RRs is in Supplementary Table S28.

## Supplementary figures

All Supplementary Figures are regenerated in final format (PDF vector and 300-dpi PNG, 180 mm width, Arial, Okabe–Ito palette, open markers where fewer than 100 patients are at risk) by `python/FIG_manuscript_final.py`. Conventions as in the main-figure conventions above.

**Supplementary Figure S1. Cohort flow.** File: SuppFigS1.pdf / .png. (A) Selection of the colorectal neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) cohort from SEER 17 (diagnoses 2000–2023). The 45,829 exported colorectal neuroendocrine neoplasm records (45,341 patients; ICD-O-3 8013, 8041–8045, 8240–8246, 8249) were filtered in sequence (malignant behavior; age ≥ 18 years; primary site C18.0, C18.2–C18.9, C19.9, C20.9; positive histology; primary by international rules; not autopsy- or death-certificate-only; diagnosis 2000–2023; survival days known; one record per patient), leaving 32,620 patients, of whom 1,417 had NEC histology (small-cell family 8041–8045, n = 871; large-cell 8013, n = 546). Stage at diagnosis: distant 908 (primary analysis cohort), regional 346, localized 116, unknown 47. The pre-specified expanded definition adds 8246/3 with registry grade 3 or 4 (1,762 of 4,619 such records; expanded cohort n = 3,179, distant n = 1,975). (B) Comparator cohorts built with the same filters from separate exports: record and patient counts at export and after filtering, the histology restriction (SCLC 8041–8045, n = 109,315; lung LCNEC 8013, n = 6,136; pancreatic adenocarcinoma excluding 8150–8157, n = 136,197; colorectal adenocarcinoma n = 737,816) and the distant-stage subsets used in the cross-tumor analyses (SCLC 77,707; lung LCNEC 3,185; colorectal adenocarcinoma 147,014; pancreatic adenocarcinoma 75,634). Step counts are those of Supplementary Table S4; a small number of patients appear in both the NEC and a comparator cohort (SCLC 1, colorectal adenocarcinoma 3) and were retained.

**Supplementary Figure S2. Change in the 12-month cancer-death probability from landmark 0 to landmark 6 months by patient stratum, distant colorectal NEC (added at revision).** File: SuppFigS2.pdf / .png. Forest plot of the landmark 6 minus landmark 0 change (later minus earlier; negative = decline) in the whole cohort (diamond; +0.03 pp, 95% CI −4.05 to 4.24 in this run; dashed line and shaded band) and within strata of primary-site surgery, liver metastasis and metastatic pattern (diagnoses 2010 and later with recorded sites), age, primary site, sex, histological subtype, race/ethnicity and recorded chemotherapy (Supplementary Table S18). For the two treatment strata, which are defined by information recorded at or after diagnosis and are therefore exposed to immortal-time bias, the contrast shown is landmark 6 minus landmark 3 months among patients alive at 3 months (Supplementary Table S19). 95% percentile intervals from patient-level bootstrap (B = 2,000 per stratum; seed 20261007 with one independent stream per stratum); open markers denote fewer than 100 patients at risk at the later landmark. **Supplementary Figure S3. Temporal split-sample validation: derivation (2000–2015) and confirmation (2016–2023) periods, distant colorectal NEC (added at revision).** File: SuppFigS3.pdf / .png. Monthly landmark curves (0–24 months) of the 12-month cancer-death probability for patients diagnosed in 2000–2015 (derivation, n = 431; B = 2,000, seed 20261007) and 2016–2023 (confirmation, n = 477; seed 20261008). Lines join point estimates; error bars are 95% bootstrap percentile intervals at the pre-specified landmarks 0, 3, 6 and 12 months; points with fewer than 20 patients at risk are suppressed and open markers denote fewer than 100 at risk; numbers at risk are shown below the panel. The 6 − 0 change is +0.34 pp (−5.58 to 5.87) in the derivation and −0.57 pp (−6.23 to 4.93) in the confirmation period (difference −0.91 pp, −8.62 to 6.98; bootstrap P = 0.82); broken-stick plateau ends are 8.5 (4.25–11.75) and 5.5 (0.0–9.0) months. The adjusted 6–9 versus 3–6-month rate ratio is 1.20 (0.83–1.72) in 2000–2015 and 1.81 (1.30–2.52) in 2016–2023 (ratio 1.52, 0.93–2.49, P = 0.094): no 0–6-month decline is detected in either half (neither half alone excludes an adenocarcinoma-sized decline), whereas the 6–9-month hazard rise replicates in direction but with its magnitude concentrated in 2016–2023. This is a temporal, within-registry validation; the later period has shorter follow-up and its late landmarks rest on few patients (Supplementary Table S25).

**Supplementary Figure S4. Dependence of the landmark curve on the width of the risk window, distant colorectal NEC (added at revision).** File: SuppFigS4.pdf / .png. Six panels, (A)–(F), show the conditional probability of cancer death within 3, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 24 months after each landmark (landmarks 0–18 months; n = 908; 95% bootstrap bands, B = 2,000, seed 20261007, identical resamples across windows), with the broken-stick fit and its change-point (11.75, 9.25, 7.75, 6.75, 6.75 and 5.75 months respectively; Supplementary Table S13). Under the 3-month window the curve falls from landmark 0 to 3 (−10.3 pp, 95% CI −15.0 to −5.5) and rises from 3 to 6 (+9.2 pp, 3.2 to 15.1); under windows of 9 months or more the 0–6-month segment is flat. The flat 12-month curve is therefore the running integral of a non-monotone short-term hazard (dip at 3–6 and rebound at 6–9 months), and the change-point location is window-dependent even though a change-point of at least 3 months is found in at least 99% of resamples under every window (P(c ≥ 3); Supplementary Table S13). Landmarks with fewer than 100 patients at risk (16 months and beyond) are drawn with open markers.

**Supplementary Figure S5. Kernel-smoothed cause-specific hazard of cancer death in distant colorectal NEC at three bandwidths (added at revision).** File: SuppFigS5.pdf / .png. Smoothed hazard (deaths per person-month; Epanechnikov kernel, boundary-corrected, muhaz) over 0–24 months at half (0.742 months), automatic (1.484 months; minimum-IMSE global selection) and double (2.968 months) bandwidth, with the pointwise 95% bootstrap band at the automatic bandwidth (B = 500, seed 20260929). The trough at month 4 and the post-trough maximum at 8.0–8.75 months are present at every bandwidth; the fixed-month ratio of peak to trough hazard is 2.12 (95% CI 1.45–3.18) at half, 1.75 (1.32–2.36) at automatic and 1.36 (1.14–1.62) at double bandwidth, and the ratio of mean hazards over 6–9 versus 3–6 months (trapezoid rule) 1.53 (1.20–1.91), 1.45 (1.18–1.75) and 1.24 (1.10–1.42) (Supplementary Table S32). The window ratio is less bandwidth-sensitive than the fixed-month ratio and its lower limit stays above 1 at every bandwidth. The timing of the extrema is imprecise (trough-month 95% interval 3.5–11.5 months; peak-month 6.75–24 months) and is descriptive; months 0 and 24 are boundary estimates and fewer than 100 patients remain at risk from month 16.

**Supplementary Figure S7. Chemotherapy with time-updated exposure: landmark-specific classification and time-dependent crude rates (added at revision).** File: SuppFigS7.pdf / .png. (A) 12-month cancer-death probability by landmark (0–12 months) in the distant no-surgery/no-radiotherapy cohort (n = 449; chemotherapy recorded in 279) and in the full distant cohort (n = 908; 580), with patients classified at each landmark as "chemotherapy initiated by the landmark" or "not initiated (never or not yet)" using the SEER time from diagnosis to first-course treatment, compared with the from-diagnosis classification by recorded chemotherapy (Supplementary Table S22a). With landmark-specific classification the falling "no/unknown" curve of the from-diagnosis split (84.7% → 71.4%) is replaced by a flat "not initiated" curve (full cohort 73.0/76.1/76.6/76.2% at landmarks 0–3) and the treated-minus-untreated gap shrinks to within a few points, which identifies immortal-time/classification bias as the main source of the from-diagnosis decomposition. (B) Crude cancer-death rates per person-month in 0–3, 3–6, 6–9 and 9–12 months with person-time split at the treatment start (Supplementary Table S22b): after initiation the rate rises from 3–6 to 6–9 months (full cohort 0.076 → 0.128 per person-month, RR 1.68, 95% CI 1.27–2.21; clean cohort 0.104 → 0.140, RR 1.35, 0.92–1.98), while the not-yet-treated person-time shows an early peak and few later events (59 and 18 deaths after month 3). These are descriptive hazard shapes under treatment selection, not treatment effects; the SEER chemotherapy field under-ascertains treatment [10] and the treatment-timing field is not chemotherapy-specific.
